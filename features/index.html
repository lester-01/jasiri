Everything Jasiri can do.

An AI assistant and a real Linux computer on your Android phone. Here is the detail behind the short version.

Download for Android (Free) Back to the short version

A real computer, with an assistant that can use it.

Jasiri puts a full Linux system on your phone. You can use it yourself, like any computer. Or ask the AI assistant, and it uses the same files and programs for you.

The computer.

A real Linux system with a terminal, a package manager and your files. It runs without root, on the phone you already own.

The assistant.

Say what you want in a chat. It writes files, runs programs and installs what the task needs, and shows every step.

The control.

You decide what the assistant may do. Jasiri asks before a risky command, and you switch each tool on or off.

More than a chat.

It can remember.

The assistant can keep notes across chats, such as your preferences or facts about a project, and it can search your past chats. Memory is a switch in Settings, Tools.

Connect more tools.

Add an MCP server to give the assistant extra tools over the internet or your network, such as a calendar or a database. It uses a server’s tools only while the server is on, and only the ones you approve.

Talk to it.

Turn speech into text, and have text read aloud. Both are switches that start off.

Reach into your phone, when you allow it.

With your say-so, the assistant can read and reply to notifications, draft calendar events and messages, take a photo or read your location. Each one is a switch that starts off. Screen control, call log and SMS are in the full edition only.

Reach it from a laptop.

Install the SSH server and the same system is one command away from a computer on your network.

Take your data with you.

Save your chats and files as a file, move to a new phone, or export everything if an update ever goes wrong.

Bring your own AI.

Jasiri does not come with an AI of its own. Use a cloud service with your own key, or run a small model on the phone itself. It works with OpenRouter and with other services that use the OpenAI-compatible interface (OpenAI and your own server are in beta). That service may charge you, and you pick the model.

An API key is like a password you get from the service.

Local models, said straight

Running a model on the phone is experimental. Small models work for short questions and simple tasks, and they struggle with long or complicated ones. Jasiri shows whether a model fits your phone’s memory before you download it.

You set the rules.

It asks before anything risky.

Before Jasiri runs a command that could do harm, it stops and shows you the command, in plain words, and waits for your answer. A few things, such as wiping your files, are always refused.

Prefer to move faster? Power users can switch the questions off.

And if something goes, you can get it back.

When the assistant deletes files with the usual delete command, they do not vanish. They go to a recycle bin and stay for 30 days. You can restore them or empty the bin from Settings. The assistant cannot empty it.

Tools are switches.

Reach into the phone, such as photos, the microphone or other apps, stays off until you turn it on.

Health check.

One screen shows what the phone needs for Jasiri to keep running, and how to fix it.

Keys are sealed.

Your API keys and tokens are stored sealed with your phone’s secure storage.

Updates are careful.

When a new version moves your data, it keeps the old copy, shows progress, and can resume if it is stopped.

Ask. Watch it work. Or do it yourself.

The pocket computer includes a Linux system. That is where programs and files live. The terminal is the text screen for that system. You can leave it closed.

  1. Ask

    Say what you want in a chat. A chat is one conversation. Attach a photo or a document when it helps.

  2. Watch it work

    The AI assistant writes files, runs them, and installs a program when the task needs one. You can see each step.

  3. Do it yourself

    Open the terminal when you want the same Linux system, and the same files, under your own hands.

The terminal is there when you want it

Type jasiri for a short guide, including the line that reaches this phone from a laptop. Installing a program from here is the same Linux system the AI assistant uses.

$ jasiri
# a short guide, and how a laptop can reach this phone
$ apt install python git

Two editions

Both editions are a pocket computer with an AI assistant built in: the terminal, a browser inside the app, skills, and automations.

This website offers the full edition. It also has tools a Play edition will not be allowed to include. Those tools can control other apps on the screen, see and open every app you have installed, and install an app file, including an update to Jasiri. When Play is the place you got Jasiri, Play is also the place that updates it.

Choose this website if you want those tools. The Play edition is enough if you want the AI assistant, the terminal, and the Linux system. Play is not listed yet. The download on this page is the full edition.

The two editions share one identity on the phone. You cannot keep both installed. Moving from one to the other later means installing again.

More questions

What can the assistant touch?

The chat, the Linux system and the project it works in. Photos, the microphone, notifications and other reaches into the phone stay off until you allow them. Controlling other apps is one of those (full edition only).

Does it work offline?

The Linux system and your files work without a connection. A cloud AI needs to reach its service. A model on the phone works without a connection.

Why does Jasiri stop when I leave it?

Some phone makers close apps they think you are not using, which can stop a long task. The Health screen in Jasiri shows what your phone needs and how to fix it. The site dontkillmyapp.com lists the steps for many phone makers.
